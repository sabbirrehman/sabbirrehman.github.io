# The Silk Road wasn’t one road
category: History
minutes: 3

It was a web of routes linking China, Central Asia, India and the Mediterranean. Hardly anyone travelled it from end to end.

Goods passed from hand to hand, and ideas travelled with them: paper, religions, recipes and, sadly, diseases too.

# Why the sky is blue
category: Science
minutes: 2

Sunlight looks white, but it holds every colour. Air molecules scatter short blue wavelengths far more than long red ones, so blue reaches your eyes from every direction.

At sunset the light crosses much more air. The blue is scattered away on the way, and the reds and oranges are what remain.

# Read one page, slowly
category: Books
minutes: 2

Pick a book you’ve been avoiding. Read a single page, as if nobody is waiting for you to finish.

Notice the sentence you want to keep. That is usually where the book begins.

# Where the rivers meet the sea

Bangladesh sits on the delta of the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna, one of the largest river deltas on Earth.

# The tiger that swims
category: Wildlife
minutes: 3

The Sundarbans, shared by Bangladesh and India, is the largest mangrove forest in the world and home to the Bengal tiger.

These tigers swim between tidal islands, which is rare for big cats. The forest is half land, half water, and they live in both.
